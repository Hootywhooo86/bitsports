Oilers Line Combinations

The Oilers entered the 2025-26 NHL season with another roster built around their superstar talent, but plenty of moving pieces throughout the lineup. Below, we’re breaking down Edmonton’s forward lines, defence pairings, and goaltending depth chart to give you a better idea of how the team is being deployed.

Forward

When you’ve got Connor McDavid and Leon Draisaitl leading the charge, you’re already starting from a pretty good place. The Oilers have two of the best offensive players on the planet, and figuring out the right combinations around them remains one of the biggest decisions facing the coaching staff. With Zach Hyman and Ryan Nugent-Hopkins providing established options in the top six, Edmonton has no shortage of firepower when its best players are rolling.

The 2025-26 season also brought some interesting changes to the forward group, with younger players like Matt Savoie looking to establish themselves and additions like Jack Roslovic providing another option in the lineup. Vasily Podkolzin continued to offer a useful combination of speed, physicality, and versatility, giving the coaching staff flexibility when building its lines. The biggest question isn’t whether Edmonton has enough star power to score goals, but rather which combinations can provide the most consistent production throughout the lineup.

Defence

While Edmonton’s forwards tend to get most of the attention, the defence group has an equally important job in helping the Oilers play the kind of hockey needed to win. Evan Bouchard and Mattias Ekholm remain a major part of that equation, with Bouchard’s ability to move the puck and contribute offensively complementing Ekholm’s steady, experienced approach in his own end.

Beyond the top pairing, the Oilers have spent plenty of time looking for the right balance between offensive production and defensive responsibility. Darnell Nurse and Jake Walman have both played important minutes, while the addition of Connor Murphy in 2025-26 gave the team another defensive-minded option. Finding dependable pairings that can handle difficult matchups remains especially important for a team with Stanley Cup aspirations.

Goalies

Goaltending has been one of the biggest talking points surrounding the Oilers over the last few seasons, and 2025-26 was no exception. After entering the season with Stuart Skinner and Calvin Pickard in the picture, Edmonton made a significant change by sending Skinner to Pittsburgh in a deal that brought Tristan Jarry to town. Connor Ingram also worked his way into the conversation, giving the Oilers a different look between the pipes as the season progressed.

For a team with legitimate championship expectations, getting reliable goaltending is an enormous piece of the puzzle. Edmonton doesn’t necessarily need its starter to steal every game, but having someone who can make the timely saves and give the team a chance to win is absolutely essential. Keeping an eye on the Oilers’ projected starting goalie is especially important for fantasy managers and anyone following the team’s upcoming matchups.

Power Play

With Connor McDavid, Leon Draisaitl, Evan Bouchard, and the rest of Edmonton’s offensive weapons available, it’s no surprise that the Oilers’ power play continues to be one of the team’s greatest strengths. When this group gets set up in the offensive zone and starts moving the puck, there aren’t many teams in the NHL that can match the creativity and talent they bring to the table.

The Oilers finished the 2025-26 regular season with a 30.6% power play, the best mark in the NHL. Edmonton’s top unit typically features four forwards and one defenceman, with Bouchard quarterbacking the attack from the blue line while McDavid and Draisaitl look to create opportunities. For fantasy hockey players and bettors alike, tracking who’s getting time on the top power play unit can be just as important as knowing the even-strength line combinations.

Penalty Kill

As much fun as it is watching the Oilers work their magic with the man advantage, the other side of special teams has been a different story. Edmonton’s penalty kill has had its share of difficult stretches, and finding ways to keep the puck out of the net while shorthanded remains an important part of the team’s overall success.

The Oilers finished the 2025-26 regular season with a 77.8% penalty kill, leaving plenty of room for improvement. Players like Ryan Nugent-Hopkins and Matt Savoie have contributed to the shorthanded group, while the coaching staff has continued looking for combinations that can win faceoffs, disrupt passing lanes, and clear the puck when opportunities arise. Penalty-killing assignments are also worth watching for fantasy managers whose leagues reward shorthanded points or other defensive statistics.

Injuries

Injuries are an unfortunate reality of every NHL season, and they can have a significant impact on how the Oilers put their lineup together. Losing a player from the top six can force a complete reshuffling of the forward lines, while an injury on the blue line can change everything from defensive pairings to special teams assignments.

That’s why it’s worth checking Edmonton’s injury report and projected lineup before every game. A player moving up alongside McDavid or Draisaitl because of an injury could suddenly become a much more interesting fantasy option, while a missing regular on the power play could open the door for someone else to earn valuable minutes.

How often do the Oilers change their lines?

The Oilers change their lines from game-to-game. Whether it’s due to a strategic reason or roster changes such as injuries or trades, the Oilers coaching staff try to ensure that they have a competitive advantage every game so changes to their lines happen often.

The Oilers top power play unit consists of Zach Hyman, Ryan Nugent-Hopkins, and Leon Draisaitl as forwards, with Evan Bouchard and Connor McDavid as defenceman. How do injuries affect the Oilers’ line combinations?

Injuries have a major effect on the Oilers line combinations. If a player is ruled out of an NHL game due to injury, this can lead to the entirety of the Oilers lines tonight changing as they need to adjust for the overall shift in their roster.
